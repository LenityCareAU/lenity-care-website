How we work

Care that fits your life, not the other way around

Three ways we bring our values to life, every day.

Caring

Support that starts with listening. We take time to understand your goals, routines and what matters most, then, where we can, match you with workers who fit, including workers who share your language or cultural background.

Going Out

Life happens in the community. We support you to get out and about for shopping, appointments, social groups, sport, worship and cultural events, so you can build connections and confidence at your own pace.

Independence

Your goals lead the way. Whether it's learning to travel on your own, managing your home, or building skills for work and study, we work alongside you step by step so you can do more of what you want, your way.

Why choose Lenity

A local team you can rely on

Finding the right provider can feel overwhelming. We make it simple, personal and consistent.

  • Matched to you. Where we can, we choose workers for your language, culture and personality, not just availability.
  • One consistent team. We aim to keep the same familiar faces around you, so you're not re-telling your story.
  • Clear communication. Plain-language plans, regular check-ins, and we aim to reply quickly to families and coordinators.
  • Local and responsive. Based in Blacktown, supporting people across Western Sydney and South Western Sydney.
  • Quality you can trust. NDIS registered, aligned with the NDIS Practice Standards, with safeguarding built into everything we do.

Support Coordination

Support Coordination helps you understand your NDIS plan and get the most out of it. Your Support Coordinator works with you to find the right providers, get your supports working together and build your confidence to manage your plan over time.

Who it's for

People who have Support Coordination funded in their NDIS plan. Not sure if you have it? We can look at your plan with you.

What's included

  • Explaining your plan, your budgets and what you can use them for, in plain language
  • Finding providers who suit your needs, culture and language
  • Setting up service agreements so your supports can start
  • Linking your NDIS supports with health, housing and community services
  • Helping you when things change, like a move, a hospital stay or a new need
  • Getting ready for your next plan, including the reports and evidence you need

How it helps

You spend less time chasing providers and more time on your goals. Step by step, you learn how your plan works, so you can make more of your own choices.

Related supports: Psychosocial Recovery Coaching and Accommodation and Living

What to expect

  1. Welcome meeting: we go through your plan with you, in plain language.
  2. Action plan: we agree on what matters most and who does what.
  3. Connections: we introduce you to providers and help set up agreements.
  4. Check-ins: regular catch-ups to track progress and solve problems.
  5. Your next plan: we help you gather evidence and get ready.

Funding

Capacity Building: Support Coordination. Our prices follow the NDIS Pricing Arrangements and Price Limits. We can show you how your budget is tracking at each check-in.

Can I choose my own providers?
Always. We give you options and information, and you make the choice.
Can Lenity provide my other supports too?
Yes, if you choose us. Your Support Coordinator will still give you other options, so the choice stays with you.
Do you work with plan managers and LACs?
Yes. With your consent, we work with your plan manager, Local Area Coordinator (LAC) and other professionals.

Psychosocial Recovery Coaching

Recovery Coaching is for people living with psychosocial disability related to a mental health condition. Your Recovery Coach walks alongside you to build hope and resilience, set recovery goals and put the right supports in place, at your pace and on your terms.

Who it's for

People with psychosocial disability who have Psychosocial Recovery Coaching funded in their NDIS plan. If you are not sure, we can check your plan with you.

What's included

  • A recovery plan built on your strengths and what matters to you
  • Help to understand and use your NDIS plan
  • Linking your NDIS supports with clinical and community mental health services
  • Building confidence, decision-making and daily living skills
  • A clear plan for tougher days, with extra support when things are hard
  • Reconnecting with community, study, work or the things you enjoy

How it helps

Coaching is practical and hopeful. You choose the goals, and your coach helps you take steps towards them. Our approach is recovery-oriented, trauma-informed and strengths-based. Sessions can be in person, by phone or by video.

Related supports: Support Coordination and Community Participation

What to expect

  1. Getting to know you: your story, your strengths and your hopes.
  2. Recovery plan: small, meaningful goals that you choose.
  3. Regular coaching: practical support and encouragement.
  4. Connecting supports: clinicians, peer workers and community groups.
  5. Reviewing progress: noticing your wins and adjusting your plan.

Funding

Capacity Building: Support Coordination. Psychosocial Recovery Coaching is usually funded in this budget when it is included in your plan. Our prices follow the NDIS Pricing Arrangements and Price Limits.

Who are your coaches?
Recovery Coaches with mental health experience or lived experience of recovery, in line with NDIS requirements for the role.
Is this the same as therapy?
No. Coaching is practical, goal-focused support. It works alongside your psychologist, psychiatrist or GP.
What if I need help urgently?
Recovery Coaching is not a crisis service. If you are in danger, call 000. For support any time, day or night, call Lifeline on 13 11 14.

Daily Living Support

Daily Living Support helps you with everyday tasks at home and helps you build the skills to do more for yourself. We follow your routines and respect your culture, your privacy and the way you like things done.

Who it's for

People who would like support with personal care, meals, medication or daily routines, and who have daily living support funded in their NDIS plan.

What's included

  • Self-care and personal care, like showering, dressing and grooming
  • Support with your personal activities at home and through your day
  • Meal preparation, from planning and shopping lists to cooking
  • Medication support: prompts and assistance in line with your medication plan
  • Life skills development, like cooking, budgeting and keeping track of appointments
  • Routines that help your day run smoothly

How it helps

You can stay safe and comfortable at home and build the confidence to do more on your own. Our workers do things with you, not just for you, so your skills can grow over time.

Related supports: Household Tasks and Community Participation

What to expect

  1. Getting to know you: your routines, your likes and your goals.
  2. Your support plan: what help you want, and when.
  3. Matching: a worker who suits you, including language and culture where we can.
  4. Regular support: at the times set out in your service agreement.
  5. Check-ins: we review how things are going and make changes.

Funding

Core Supports: Assistance with Daily Life. Help to build life skills may be funded from your Capacity Building budget, depending on your plan. We explain our prices in your service agreement before we start.

What does medication support involve?
Our workers can remind you and help you take your medication, following the medication plan from your doctor or pharmacist. They do not change your medication or give medical advice.
Can I choose who supports me?
Yes. Tell us what matters to you, such as gender, language or culture, and we will do our best to match you.

Household Tasks

Household Tasks support helps keep your home clean, safe and comfortable. We help with the jobs your disability makes hard to do, so you have more time and energy for the things you enjoy.

Who it's for

People who find some household jobs hard to do because of their disability, and who have household tasks funded in their NDIS plan.

What's included

  • Cleaning, like vacuuming, mopping, bathrooms and kitchens
  • Laundry, including washing, drying, folding and changing bed linen
  • Yard maintenance, keeping outdoor areas safe and tidy
  • Gardening and lawn mowing
  • Bin days: putting your bins out and bringing them back in
  • Help to learn household skills, if that is one of your goals

How it helps

A clean, tidy home is safer and easier to live in. You do the jobs you want to do, and we help with the rest.

Related supports: Daily Living Support and Home Modifications

What to expect

  1. A chat at home: what needs doing and how you like it done.
  2. Your task list: the jobs, and how often they happen.
  3. Regular visits: on the days and times you agree to.
  4. Check-ins: we update your list if your needs change.

Funding

Core Supports: Assistance with Daily Life. Household tasks are funded when your plan includes them, usually because your disability makes these jobs hard to do yourself. Our prices follow the NDIS Pricing Arrangements and Price Limits.

Is lawn mowing covered by the NDIS?
It can be, if yard maintenance is included in your plan. We can help you check.
Will the same worker come each time?
That is our aim. Familiar faces in your home matter, so we try to keep your team small and consistent.

Community Participation

Community Participation support helps you get out, try new things and feel part of your community. We support you to go where you want to go, meet people, and build friendships and confidence.

Who it's for

People who want support to take part in social, community and recreation activities, and who have this support funded in their NDIS plan.

What's included

  • Shopping and getting your groceries
  • Going to appointments, with support before, during and after
  • Outings, events, cultural activities and places of worship
  • Recreation, sport and fitness activities
  • Group and centre activities where you can meet new people
  • Building friendships, social skills and confidence

How it helps

Being part of your community can lift your mood, grow your skills and open new doors. We go at your pace, and you make the choices.

Related supports: Travel and Transport and Personal Training

What to expect

  1. Your interests: what you love, and what you would like to try.
  2. Your goals: like making friends, joining a group or getting out more.
  3. Planning: activities, times and how you will get there.
  4. Getting out: with a worker who shares your interests, where we can.
  5. Review: we check how it is going and try new ideas.

Funding

Core Supports: Assistance with Social, Economic and Community Participation. Some skill building may be funded from Capacity Building: Increased Social and Community Participation. The NDIS does not usually pay for everyday costs like tickets, entry fees or meals.

Can I join a group activity?
Yes. We can help you find and join group and centre activities that match your interests.
Can my worker help me get there?
Yes. Transport linked to your activities can often be claimed from your plan, and we explain any costs in your service agreement. See Travel and Transport.

Travel and Transport

Travel and Transport support helps you get where you need to go. We can support you to get to appointments, work, study and activities, and help you learn to travel on your own.

Who it's for

People who find it hard to get around or use public transport because of their disability, and who have transport or travel support in their NDIS plan.

What's included

  • Getting to and from appointments
  • Getting to work, study and volunteering
  • Getting to social, sport and community activities
  • Travel training to use buses and trains, step by step
  • Planning trips, reading timetables and using your Opal card
  • Building the confidence and safety skills to travel alone

How it helps

Getting around opens up your whole week. With travel training, you can learn to travel more on your own, when you feel ready.

Related supports: Community Participation and Daily Living Support

What to expect

  1. Your trips: where you need to go, and how often.
  2. The right option: a worker driving you, or travelling with you on public transport.
  3. Travel training: if you want to learn, we practise your routes together.
  4. More independence: we step back as your confidence grows.

Funding

Core Supports: Transport. Transport linked to your activities may also be claimed with Core Supports: Assistance with Social, Economic and Community Participation. Travel training may be funded from your Capacity Building budget. We explain all transport costs in your service agreement before we start.

What is travel training?
Travel training teaches you how to travel on your own, often by bus or train. We practise your route with you until you feel confident.
Will a worker drive me?
Where it is part of your support, a worker may drive you. Any costs for kilometres travelled are set out in your service agreement.

Accommodation and Living

Accommodation and Living support helps you find the right place to live and the support to live well there. We can help you arrange short stays and respite, Supported Independent Living (SIL) and support with your tenancy and home.

Who it's for

People who want a short break away from home, would like support to live more independently, or need help to find and keep a home. What we can arrange depends on your NDIS plan.

What's included

  • Help to find and arrange Short-Term Accommodation (STA) and respite
  • Supported Independent Living (SIL): support with daily tasks in the home where you live
  • Tenancy support, like understanding your lease and talking with your landlord
  • Accommodation support, including help to look for housing that suits you
  • House and household management, like bills, budgets and routines
  • Links to Home Modifications if your home needs changes

How it helps

The right home and the right support make everyday life easier. A short stay can give you a break, a change of scene and a chance to try new things, and it can give your family or carers a rest too.

Related supports: Home Modifications and Daily Living Support

What to expect

  1. Talking it through: your living situation, your needs and your goals.
  2. Exploring options: STA, respite, SIL or tenancy support.
  3. Arranging it: we can help with bookings, paperwork and quotes.
  4. Settling in: support as you start, and check-ins after.

Funding

Core Supports: Assistance with Daily Life funds STA, respite and SIL when they are in your plan. Help to find and keep a home may be funded from Capacity Building: Improved Living Arrangements. We can help you understand what your plan includes.

What is the difference between STA and SIL?
STA is a short stay away from home with support. SIL is ongoing support with daily tasks in the home where you live.
Can my carer have a break?
Yes. Respite through STA can give your family or carers a break while you enjoy a short stay with support.
How do I get SIL in my plan?
The NDIA decides on SIL funding after looking at your home and living needs. We can explain the process and what information may help.

Home Modifications

Home Modifications make your home safer and easier to live in. From grab rails and ramps to bathroom and kitchen changes, we coordinate the whole process: assessment, approval, building work and follow-up.

Who it's for

People whose disability makes it hard to move around or use parts of their home safely, and who have (or need an assessment for) Home Modifications funding.

What's included

  • Grab rails, handrails and hand-held showers
  • Ramps, step changes and level thresholds
  • Accessible bathrooms and level-access showers
  • Wider doorways, and changes to kitchen benches and taps
  • Lighting, automatic doors and other safety improvements
  • Coordinating the assessment, quotes, approvals and trades for you

How it helps

Well-designed changes can reduce falls, make daily tasks easier and help you stay in the home and community you know.

Related supports: Accommodation and Living and Support Coordination

What to expect

  1. Assessment: we can help arrange an Occupational Therapist (OT) to assess your home and needs.
  2. Planning and quotes: a scope of works, quotes and NDIA approval where needed.
  3. Building work: done by independent licensed tradespeople and coordinated by us.
  4. Handover and follow-up: we check that everything works for you.

Funding

Capital Supports: Home Modifications. Most changes need an OT assessment first. Larger or more complex changes also need NDIA approval before work starts. We guide you through each step.

I rent my home. Can I still get modifications?
Often, yes, with your landlord's permission. We can help you with that conversation.
Do you do the building work yourselves?
No. Independent licensed tradespeople do the work, and we coordinate the process for you.

Personal Training

Build strength, fitness and confidence in a way that works for your body and your goals. Sessions are adapted to your abilities and can happen at home, in the community or at a local gym.

Who it's for

People with health and wellbeing goals, such as moving more easily, building strength, managing weight, or using exercise to support mood and sleep.

What's included

  • A program built around your goals, your body and your energy levels
  • Work on mobility, balance and preventing falls
  • Strength for daily tasks and independence
  • Energy, mood and a healthy weekly routine
  • Confidence to use gyms, pools and community sport
  • Sessions adapted for wheelchair users, sensory needs and chronic health conditions

How it helps

Regular exercise can make everyday tasks easier and help you feel good. Where it helps, we work with your physiotherapist or exercise physiologist.

Related supports: Community Participation and Daily Living Support

What to expect

  1. Goal chat: what you want to be able to do.
  2. Gentle start: we find out where you are starting from, safely.
  3. Your program: sessions that fit your week and your body.
  4. Tracking progress: small wins, celebrated.

Funding

Depending on your plan and goals. Personal training may be funded from Capacity Building: Improved Health and Wellbeing, and how your plan is managed also makes a difference. We can help you check before you start.

Who are your trainers?
Qualified personal trainers with experience supporting people with disability.
Do I need to be fit to start?
No. We start where you are, at a pace that feels safe and comfortable.

Our story

Built in Blacktown, for our community

Lenity Care Australia was founded in 2018 with a simple idea: disability support works best when it fits the whole person: their culture, their language, their family and their goals.

Western Sydney is one of the most diverse communities in Australia, and too many people were being asked to fit into services rather than the other way around. We set out to change that by matching participants with workers who understand them, keeping teams consistent, and communicating clearly with families and professionals.

Today we support people across Western Sydney and South Western Sydney with daily living support, household tasks, community participation, travel and transport, accommodation and living support, Support Coordination, Psychosocial Recovery Coaching, Home Modifications and Personal Training.

For NDIS participants

Supports that put you in control

You choose what support you want, who provides it and how. Here's what working with Lenity looks like.

What we offer you

  • Workers matched to your language, culture and personality, where we can
  • Nine support areas under one roof, so supports work together
  • A consistent team and a named contact person, where possible
  • Plain-language agreements and clear budgets

How to access our services

  1. Contact us by phone, email or the form, or ask your LAC or Support Coordinator to refer you.
  2. We meet, talk about your goals and check what your plan covers.
  3. We agree a service agreement and match you with your team.

Your rights and safeguards

Under the NDIS Code of Conduct you have the right to be treated with respect, to make your own decisions, to privacy, to safe and competent support, and to speak up without fear. We take these seriously. See our Safeguarding commitment.

Feedback and complaints

Tell us what's working and what isn't, by phone, email, our feedback form or through a family member or advocate. You can also contact the NDIS Commission directly on 1800 035 544. Read our complaints process.

About the NDIS and getting started

What is the NDIS?

The National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) is the Australian Government scheme that funds supports for eligible people with permanent and significant disability. Your funding is set out in your NDIS plan, and you choose the providers you want to work with. You can call the NDIS on 1800 800 110.

How do I get started with Lenity?

Call us on 0499 938 844, email info@lenity.com.au or send a message through our contact page. We will talk about your goals, check what your plan covers and set up a service agreement with you.

How soon supports can start depends on the service, your plan and finding the right worker for you.

Can someone else refer me?

Yes. A family member, carer, Support Coordinator, Local Area Coordinator (LAC), health professional or other provider can refer you, with your consent. They can use our referral page. You can also refer yourself.

I don't have an NDIS plan yet. Can you still help?

Yes. We can explain how the NDIS works, how to apply and who to talk to. You can call the NDIS on 1800 800 110 to ask about applying. Once you have a plan, we can talk with you about the supports that fit.

Which areas do you cover?

We are based in Blacktown and support people across Western Sydney and South Western Sydney. If you are not sure whether we can reach you, just ask.

Our services

What services do you offer?

We offer nine services: Support Coordination, Psychosocial Recovery Coaching, Daily Living Support, Household Tasks, Community Participation, Travel and Transport, Accommodation and Living, Home Modifications and Personal Training. See all our services.

What is the difference between Support Coordination and Recovery Coaching?

Both help you use your plan well. Support Coordination focuses on finding and coordinating providers across all your supports. Psychosocial Recovery Coaching is for people with psychosocial disability, and it combines coordination with recovery-focused coaching. Your plan will say which one is funded.

What help can I get with daily living?

Daily Living Support can include personal care, meal preparation, daily routines and building life skills. It can also include medication support, which means prompts and assistance in line with your medication plan. Read more about Daily Living Support.

Can you help with cleaning, laundry and the garden?

Yes. Our Household Tasks service can include cleaning, laundry, yard maintenance, gardening, lawn mowing and bin days. These are funded when your plan includes them, usually because your disability makes these jobs hard to do yourself. Read more about Household Tasks.

Can a support worker take me to appointments, shopping or activities?

Yes. Community Participation support can help with shopping, appointments, outings, sport, recreation and group activities. Travel and Transport support can help you get there, and travel training can help you learn to use public transport. We explain any transport costs in your service agreement.

What are short-term accommodation (STA) and respite?

Short-Term Accommodation (STA) is a short stay away from home, with support. It can give you a break and a chance to try new things, and it can give your family or carers a rest. This is often called respite. We can help you find and arrange STA when it is included in your plan. Read more about Accommodation and Living.

What is Supported Independent Living (SIL)?

SIL is ongoing help with daily tasks in the home where you live, so you can be as independent as possible. Many people who get SIL share a home with others. The NDIA decides on SIL funding after looking at your home and living needs.

We can also help with tenancy support and managing your home. See Accommodation and Living.

Do you support people with a specific type of disability?

We support people with many types of disability, including intellectual disability, autism, psychosocial disability, physical disability and acquired brain injury. Tell us about your needs, and we will be honest about whether we are the right fit.

Can my family or carer be involved?

Yes, as much as you want them to be. With your consent, we include family and carers in planning, updates and reviews. See Families and Carers.

How much do your services cost?

Our prices follow the NDIS Pricing Arrangements and Price Limits, and we set them out in your service agreement before supports begin. Most people pay from their NDIS plan, whether it is NDIA-managed, plan-managed or self-managed. Personal Training funding depends on your plan and goals, so we help you check first.

Can I change or cancel my supports?

Yes. Your service agreement explains notice periods and any short-notice cancellation charges, which follow the NDIS Pricing Arrangements. You can end your agreement at any time in line with its terms. If a worker is not the right fit for you, tell us and we will work on a change.

Privacy, safety and feedback

How is my privacy protected?

We collect only the information we need, store it securely and share it only with your consent or when the law requires it. You can ask to see or correct your information at any time. Read our Privacy Policy.

How do I give feedback or make a complaint?

How do you keep people safe?

If you are in danger, call 000. You can also call the National Disability Abuse and Neglect Hotline on 1800 880 052.

Your safety

Safeguarding Commitment

Everyone we support has the right to be safe, respected and free from abuse, neglect and exploitation.

Summary of our Safeguarding Policy · Full policy available on request

Our commitment

Lenity Care Australia has zero tolerance for abuse, neglect, exploitation, discrimination or violence towards any person we support. We follow the NDIS Code of Conduct, the NDIS Practice Standards and the NDIS (Incident Management and Reportable Incidents) Rules.

How we keep people safe

  • Screening: all workers hold an NDIS Worker Screening Check and, where relevant, a Working with Children Check.
  • Training: induction, the NDIS Worker Orientation Module and ongoing training in safeguarding, rights and respectful practice.
  • Supervision: regular supervision and support for staff, and thoughtful matching of workers to participants.
  • Incident management: every incident is recorded, reviewed and acted on. Reportable incidents are notified to the NDIS Commission within required timeframes.
  • Restrictive practices: used only when authorised, as a last resort, with a behaviour support plan, and reported as required.
  • Listening: regular check-ins, easy ways to give feedback, and support to access advocates.

Prohibited conduct

Our workers must never use physical, verbal, emotional, sexual or financial abuse; neglect a person's needs; take money, gifts or property; have inappropriate relationships with participants; or ignore or cover up concerns. Breaches lead to disciplinary action and reporting to authorities.

You can also contact the NDIS Commission (1800 035 544) or the National Disability Abuse and Neglect Hotline (1800 880 052). You will be supported, not blamed, for speaking up.
